- Main question: was the Roman Republic worth preserving, and did Caesar actually destroy it
- SPQR = Senatus Populusque Romanus, a motto of the Roman Republic
- Two classes: patricians (small group of elite families) and plebeians (everyone else)
- Senate = legislature + advisory council, chosen from elite families, set policy for the consuls
- Senate picked 2 co-consuls each year as chief executives, so they could check each other (one handles Rome, one fights wars)
- Dictator = temporary position when the Republic was in danger
- Caesar born ~100 BCE to a leading senatorial family
- Served in the army and Senate, was governor of Spain, then ran for consul
- Crassus (one of the richest men in Rome) funded his run
- Caesar became consul in 59 BCE
- First Triumvirate = Caesar + Crassus + Pompey
- After being consul, Caesar became governor of Gaul and conquered the rest of it, legions became his power base
- Also invaded Britain
- Crassus died fighting the Parthians, Pompey became Caesar's rival and was elected consul
- Pompey and the Senate tried to strip Caesar's command and bring him back to Rome to be prosecuted
- By 48 BCE Caesar was dictator and consul, controlled all of Rome's territory
- Pompey fled to Egypt and was assassinated by Pharaoh Ptolemy's people
- Caesar sided with Cleopatra in Egypt's civil war, she became the last pharaoh
- Caesar named dictator again, then for 10 years, then for life
- Consul in 46 and 45 BCE (45 with no co-consul)
- 44 BCE senators stabbed him 23 times in the Senate because he had too much power
- "Et tu, Brute" was invented by Shakespeare
- Assassins thought killing him would restore the Republic, it didn't because his reforms were popular
- Second Triumvirate = Octavian (adopted son) + Mark Antony + Lepidus, fell into civil war
- Octavian beat Antony, became Caesar Augustus, first emperor, Senate became a rubber stamp
- Caesar wasn't the first to do this:
- Empire traits (like Persia): unified government, constant military expansion, diverse subject peoples
- Rome went city -> city-state -> kingdom -> republic, took over Latins/Etruscans and all of Italy by ~4th century BCE
- Punic Wars vs Carthage
- 1st Punic War: Rome took Sicily
- 2nd Punic War: Hannibal crossed the Alps with elephants (219 BCE start), almost won, Rome got Spain
- By 201 BCE Rome was already an empire
- 3rd Punic War: Rome completely destroyed Carthage
- Rome was an empire ~200 years before Augustus became emperor
- Thesis: empire needed constant expansion to give land to retired soldiers -> created all-powerful generals
- Diverse peoples were loyal to their general, not the idea of the Republic
- Caesar didn't create emperors, empire did
